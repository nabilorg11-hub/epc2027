What is EPC?

EPC is an annual forum where young Muslims sharpen their writing, speaking and thinking while reflecting on the role of faith in today’s world. Students compete in essays and speeches, extemporaneous writing and impromptu speaking.

The top five winners in each of the six levels receive cash awards of $25 to $100, plus certificates and trophies. Their essays may be published in Young Muslim Voices, available on Amazon. Every other participant receives a certificate and a trophy.

No essay submission is required. You take part in the impromptu speech and extemporaneous writing contests on January 23, 2027.

  • DMV area: attend in person at ISB. Virtual participation is not allowed.
  • Outside the DMV: join through Zoom.

Rules for virtual participants

  • You get your prompt 5 to 10 minutes before you start, based on your grade level.
  • Keep your camera on while you prepare and compete, and work on your own.
  • Judges must see your face, upper body and both hands.
  • For the speech, you may use index cards. Your hands must stay free except when holding the cards.
  • No outside help, AI or other technology.
Disqualification. Anyone who uses AI or outside help during the impromptu speech or extemporaneous writing is disqualified.

The Art of Patience

This year’s theme: Finding Purpose in a World That Never Slows Down.

Level 1 (Grades 1–2)

Write a friendly letter to a family member or friend about a time you had to be patient. Explain what happened, how you felt while waiting and what you learned. How can patience help us in daily life? Include all the parts of a friendly letter.

Level 2 (Grades 3–4)

Our world encourages us to seek quick results, instant answers and immediate success. Why is patience still important? Discuss how it helps in school, friendships, family relationships and personal growth.

Level 3 (Grades 5–6)

Modern life rewards immediate results, rapid communication and constant productivity, yet relationships, education, leadership and growth often need patience. Has our obsession with speed caused us to undervalue patience? Consider how patience shapes decisions, relationships and long-term goals, and how faith can guide us toward a more purposeful life.

Impromptu & Extempore

Join the impromptu contest to build the confidence and quick thinking you need for MAFIQ’s premier national debate tournament.

Why impromptu speech?

Strong speaking skills open doors in any career, from business and law to medicine, public service and entrepreneurship. An impromptu contest is your chance to organize your thoughts under pressure and speak with confidence without preparation. You don’t need to be a great speaker. You just need to be willing to grow.

What you get

A certificate, and the chance to compete for finalist and winner awards.
Winners receive an annual title and may join an exclusive workshop or one-on-one session.
Personal feedback and a judging rubric to track your communication, critical thinking and confidence.
Finalists and winners may be featured on MAFIQ’s social media and in Young Muslim Voices.
Extemporaneous writing works the same way: you get a prompt for your grade level 5 to 10 minutes before you start, and you write on your own with your camera on. See the rules.
